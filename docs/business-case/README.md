Six of eleven pages answer "Yes", "Mostly" or "Yes in direction" to "Agree?". Five answer "Partly". The two views ask different questions: the company asks "should we build", the retrospective asks "did the portfolio gain". Where they differ, the retrospective usually scores the full build higher, because it counts hiring evidence.

## Three lessons for deciding the next project before building it

1. **Run the ex-ante page first and let "build small first" cap the scope.** Eight of eleven pages recommend "build small first", and the full builds that followed earned their value as hiring evidence, not as company value. Write a two-week scope with a stop rule before the first commit. Extend only when a named condition on the page is met.
2. **Name the buyer, the euro stake and the free alternative in the first line.** `funnel_correlation_py`, `Chart_Audit_Framework`, `brazil_presidential_election_prediction_2026` and `NextMove` had no company buyer. A free or cheap alternative (the R package, a checklist, a spreadsheet) existed for several. If the persona sits outside the Austrian target market, the verdict is "do nothing" or a cap of 8 h.
3. **Add a branch for hiring evidence, legal risk and reuse to every tree.** The company trees missed three things that decided the outcome: portfolio value (energy, warehouse, MMM, metric simulator), legal risk (`Chart_Audit_Framework`) and reuse value (`austria-data-job-market-intelligence`). Score the project twice before you start: once as a company tool, once with the five hiring-evidence items of the retrospective. Build only when both scores clear their bar.
